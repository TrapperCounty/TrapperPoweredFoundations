# Trapper's Powered Foundations

Satisfactory mod for TrapperCounty, based on the **Powered foundation mod idea** chat.

Version **0.1.4** adds power transfer through directly touching top and bottom foundation faces, including mixed heights and finishes. Removing a middle block splits the circuit; gaps and corner-only contact stay isolated. Existing stacks update automatically after loading.

Version **0.1.3** adds four red/green corner indicators and native Customizer finishes: FICSIT, Concrete, Coated Concrete, Asphalt and Grip Metal at all three heights. The corner lights remain independent of the surface finish. Changing finishes keeps a powered foundation, with the matching height and electrical support.

Version **0.1.2** adds electrical support for wall outlets mounted directly on a powered foundation's side. All six vanilla single/double Mk.1/Mk.2/Mk.3 variants are supported. Existing saved outlets are recognized automatically after loading. Outlets on separate walls retain the previous behavior below.

Version **0.1.1** corrects the foundation mesh's vertical offset to match vanilla. This fixes the visible step beside existing foundations and the apparent sinking through the floor. Existing saved foundations receive the correction when loaded; their actor positions and power connections are unchanged. The correction is 0.5m, 1m and 2m downward for the 1m, 2m and 4m models respectively. Pieces deliberately positioned to compensate for the old visual offset may need rebuilding.

## Playing

Buy **Powered Foundations** in the AWESOME Shop's Foundations category for **25 coupons**. The unlock contains 1m, 2m and 4m powered foundation variants in all five finishes. Their recipes preserve the vanilla ingredients and add **2 / 6 / 12 Wire**, respectively.

1. Build a floor using the powered variants. Touching side edges and directly stacked top/bottom faces conduct electricity. Corners and gaps between stories do not connect. Different thicknesses can connect along overlapping sides.
2. Put an ordinary power pole on the floor and connect a cable from your power supply. Multiple feed poles intentionally join their circuits through the floor.
3. Place machines on the floor. A machine needs some of its actual footprint resting on a powered tile. A large machine can extend beyond the floor. A wall outlet can mount directly on the foundation's side, or on a separate wall whose base rests on the floor. Connect a normal power cable to the outlet to feed the floor; an energized floor can also supply a cable attached to the outlet.
4. Green corner lights indicate energized power. Red corner lights indicate no power or a tripped fuse. Removing a connecting tile splits the circuits; replacing it reconnects them.

Hidden connections do not consume a pole's visible cable slots. Ordinary switches are excluded from automatic attachment so the floor does not bypass their switching function. Generators still supply power through normal wiring. Powered walls and ceilings are outside this version's scope.

## Architecture

- Each powered foundation owns a stable, saved `UPFPowerConnection` component. These actors disable the game's lightweight actor removal while retaining inherited foundation behavior. An explicit vanilla mesh and box collider provide rendering and a solid floor.
- The server's `APFNetworkSubsystem` maintains native hidden power links. Side contact forms the floor graph; each consumer terminal attaches to one supporting tile. A machine spanning separate islands does not create an unintended bridge.
- A spatial hash narrows contact queries. Construction and dismantle events are batched at 250ms; a periodic reconciliation catches moved objects and lightweight wall changes. Designer contents are discovered through the Designer's own building list and change event, since they bypass the ordinary factory list. Connections stay within the same Designer context.
- Vanilla circuit code performs merges, splits, consumption and fuse handling. No substitute power simulation runs in the mod.
- Only the energized indicator flag replicates from this subsystem's logic; ordinary native circuit replication remains intact. The same component names and assets are retained across saves and blueprints.

## Verification

Automated editor tests cover side/corner/gap contacts, rotated grids, different heights, machine footprint overlap, vertical face contact and gap isolation, and the saved content's recipes, unlock and replication settings.

For the packaged-game integration test, launch the game with `-PFIntegrationTest` and load a world without an existing test fixture. The opt-in test spawns an isolated fixture above the map, supplies it through real vanilla cables, checks solid collision, pole variants, a wall outlet, and large-machine contact, removes/replaces a middle tile, removes feeds and the wall, and cleans up its own objects. Its report is written to the game's `Saved/PoweredFoundations-IntegrationTest.txt`. Add `-PFShowTest` to temporarily view the fixture. Add `-PFSaveTest` to write a separately named `PF_Validation_...` save before cleanup.

To check persistence, restart with `-PFReloadTest` and load that validation save. The report is `Saved/PoweredFoundations-ReloadTest.txt`. Steam Cloud can restore archived test saves, so explicitly select the original save when rerunning the integration test. None of these tests run during ordinary play.

For exact snapping and horizontal zoop checks, launch with `-PFPlacementTest`. This opt-in test uses the game's real recipe-created holograms, build-gun collision traces, side-snap functions and zoop construction. It checks all sizes at 0 and 37 degrees, all four sides, four-tile spacing and complete material costs, and power propagation through the resulting rows. Its temporary fixtures are removed after the test; it does not deduct player inventory. The report is `Saved/PoweredFoundations-PlacementTest.txt`. This covers native placement behavior rather than a mouse-and-keyboard walkthrough of the zoop mode selector.

For vertical zooping, use `-PFVerticalZoopTest`. This checks four-tile stacks upward and downward for all three sizes at 0 and 37 degrees, their complete costs and height spacing, stable power components, and power conduction between touching tiers. It uses the native vertical mode and removes its temporary stacks. The report is `Saved/PoweredFoundations-VerticalZoopTest.txt`.

For mixed-height snapping and dismantle refunds, use `-PFMixedRefundTest`. This compares all six ordered height pairings with vanilla holograms on all four sides at 0 and 37 degrees, then verifies power across each pair. It dismantles temporary tiles of all three sizes through the native server handler, checks complete refunds arriving in player inventory, and removes those test-generated materials to restore the previous totals. Leave inventory and building controls idle while it runs. The report is `Saved/PoweredFoundations-MixedRefundTest.txt`; all remaining fixtures are removed afterward.

For maximum-length zoops and full-inventory refunds, use `-PFMaxOverflowTest`. This constructs ten-tile lines along positive and negative X/Y/Z for all three sizes at 0 and 37 degrees, compares the maximum and beyond-limit preview with vanilla, and checks costs, spacing and power behavior. Temporary filler makes player inventory completely full during a native dismantle request for one tile per size. The filler is removed immediately, then the test verifies exact overflow crate contents and native collection, removes the test-only refunds, and cleans up its tiles and empty crates. Keep inventory and building controls idle during the test. Its report is `Saved/PoweredFoundations-MaxOverflowTest.txt`.

Before a public release, also verify remote multiplayer, long normal-play sessions, and grids beyond the tested 1,024 tiles. Powered tiles retain individual actors and meshes, so they have more overhead than vanilla lightweight foundations. Consult `VALIDATION.txt` for results actually obtained on this installation.
